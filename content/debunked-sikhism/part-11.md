# Foreword

1984 is not one event. June's army operation at the Harmandir Sahib complex, Indira Gandhi's assassination on 31 October, the targeted killing of Sikhs in November and the longer period of insurgency and counter-insurgency have different actors, evidence and questions. They are connected historically. They are not interchangeable.

This distinction matters because grief can be used to hide another person's grief, and a security argument can be used to remove a civilian from view. A victim does not have to prove the innocence of every other Sikh before their murder becomes wrong. A person killed by militants does not become irrelevant because state abuses were also real. A government has duties that do not disappear because it confronts an armed threat.

I will distinguish allegations, witness accounts, commission findings and court judgments. A testimony is evidence to evaluate, not an automatic conviction. An inquiry can establish serious matters without deciding criminal guilt. A court decision concerns specified defendants, facts and charges; it is not a verdict on the entire history. A date attached to a judgment does not certify the current status of every later appeal.

The sources linked here include the Nanavati Commission, the Delhi High Court's 17 December 2018 judgment, the NHRC's dated mass-cremation account and the UK government's 2014 review. Their different limits are part of the explanation. This is an account of the cited record, not an assertion that every relevant file has been opened or every family's experience exhausted.

# Claim 01: June and November were one interchangeable incident

Operation Blue Star and the November anti-Sikh killings had the same perpetrators, circumstances and evidential record, so a fact about one settles the other.

## 1. Where It Came From

The year becomes a label that replaces the sequence. A justification offered for an army action is then used to explain attacks on people in Delhi months later. Alternatively, a finding about organised mobs in November is transferred to every specific allegation about June. The emotional connection is real; the evidential transfer is not.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

The religious importance of the Harmandir Sahib and Akal Takht helps explain the impact of June. It does not make every civilian killed in November a participant in a battle. A Sikh's relationship to those institutions must not be confused with legal or moral responsibility for someone else's acts. Shared human worth applies to each person's treatment. [SGGS1299]

## 3. The Real History

June involved a military operation against armed occupants in a sacred complex where civilians were also present. November followed the assassination and involved attacks on Sikhs in Delhi and elsewhere. Nanavati's inquiry concerns the later anti-Sikh violence; the UK review concerns alleged British involvement in planning connected with the Amritsar operation. They answer different questions. [NANAVATI] [UK-1984]

## 4. The Debunk

The interchangeability claim is false. A person can recognise connections without combining responsibility, casualties or findings. Before citing a number or accusation, identify the event and the source's coverage. A report on November cannot be described as an independently verified June casualty register merely because both concern 1984.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Put dates, places and actions beside each claim. Discuss the links afterwards. This order prevents a rationale for one operation from becoming an excuse for murdering an unrelated neighbour. It also prevents a documented finding in one case from being used to fill an evidential gap in another.

# Claim 02: Everyone inside the complex was a combatant

The presence of armed groups at the Harmandir Sahib complex proves that every person inside it during the operation was armed or responsible for the fighting.

## 1. Where It Came From

A place is given one identity, and everyone in it inherits that identity. The argument is convenient because it eliminates the civilian question before the evidence is examined. The reverse claim, that no armed occupants were there because the place was sacred, makes the same substitution of a building's status for information about its occupants.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

A sacred place's religious significance neither automatically acquits an armed actor nor makes a pilgrim an armed actor. Religious attendance is not proof of participation in hostilities. No teaching cited here removes the need to distinguish a person's actual action from the collective description assigned to the place where they were found.

## 3. The Real History

Accounts distinguish armed occupants from pilgrims and other civilians. The main assault occurred amid a period of religious observance, but a complete individual classification is not available from the sources cited in this part. Recognising civilians' presence does not establish the exact number killed, just as recognising armed resistance does not classify everyone present.

## 4. The Debunk

The universal-combatant inference is invalid. It cannot be made from location alone. The no-armed-occupants claim is likewise inconsistent with the operation's documented setting. A responsible inquiry asks who was present, what they did, what options were available and how civilian protection was addressed. Neither absolute removes those questions.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Keep the civilian in the account even when their individual circumstances remain incomplete. Assess particular actors using evidence. A serious security discussion can examine an armed threat and still question decisions that endangered noncombatants. Calling such questions anti-national or anti-Sikh contributes nothing to establishing what happened.

# Claim 03: One exact June casualty total is conclusively verified

Every person killed in Operation Blue Star is accounted for in one uncontested complete list, and any other estimate is either wholly fabricated or automatically more truthful.

## 1. Where It Came From

An official figure is treated as independent verification because it is official. A higher figure is treated as independent verification because it challenges the government. Neither description supplies the counting method. Civilian and militant deaths, the complex and wider operations, immediate deaths and later losses may also be mixed without warning.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

The human seriousness of death does not depend on choosing the largest number. Truthful living does not require accepting the smallest number either. A religious commemoration can honour victims while a historical count remains qualified. No scriptural passage converts an estimate into a census. [SGGS62]

## 3. The Real History

The cited material does not establish a complete independently verified total for June. Government accounts and other estimates need their original definitions and methods stated. Restricted contemporary access and gaps in individual documentation limit retrospective precision. The uncertainty concerns counting; it is not a reason to pretend the assault, destruction or loss of life did not occur.

## 4. The Debunk

The claim of an uncontested exhaustive count goes beyond the evidence examined here. The claim that every alternative must be true also goes beyond it. Label an official tally as an official tally and an estimate as an estimate. Do not combine unlike categories to produce an apparently stronger number.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Give the source, date, geographical coverage and categories before comparing figures. Where those details are unavailable, say so. A careful answer can establish severe harm while declining to invent exact arithmetic. Victims deserve evidence that survives inspection, not a numerical contest whose method nobody can explain.

# Claim 04: November was only spontaneous anger and nobody organised attacks

The anti-Sikh killings were simply an uncontrolled reaction to the assassination, with no organised targeting or significant police failure.

## 1. Where It Came From

The word riot is made to imply an unplanned crowd and nobody in particular making decisions. A trigger is treated as a full explanation. The assassination then becomes a supposed justification for attacking unrelated people, instead of an event followed by crimes that need their own account of organisation and responsibility.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

No shared religious identity makes a neighbour responsible for an assassin's action. The principle against treating others as permanent enemies challenges that move. The moral question is already clear before a court determines a particular defendant's guilt: a civilian does not become a legitimate target because of their religious appearance. [SGGS1299]

## 3. The Real History

Nanavati distinguished the initial reaction from the organised pattern of later attacks, and examined failures of protection. The Delhi High Court's 2018 judgment convicted specified defendants and discussed organised targeting and institutional failure. These findings defeat a description of the whole November violence as nothing beyond spontaneous anger. [NANAVATI] [DELHI-2018]

## 4. The Debunk

The only-spontaneous account is false. This does not establish the truth of every accusation or one undocumented command directing every attack. Nanavati's findings about local actors must not be misreported as a finding that the highest political leadership issued a proven order. Organised crimes and limits on an attribution can both be acknowledged.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Use language that describes targeted violence and the evidence of organisation. Distinguish a finding, a recommendation and a conviction. If using pogrom or genocide, explain the historical or legal meaning intended and its basis. A label should help the reader understand the evidence, not replace the evidence with a demand for allegiance.

# Claim 05: The assassination made Sikhs collectively guilty

Because the assassins were Sikhs, all Sikh civilians bore responsibility and attacks on them were a defensible response.

## 1. Where It Came From

Two people's actions are attached to a whole population. The visible identity makes targets easier to select than actual responsibility does. Once collective guilt is accepted, a child, shopkeeper or commuter can be treated as an accused person without any evidence linking them to a crime. That is the logic of collective punishment.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

Human worth is not inherited guilt. The scriptural rejection of hostility based on the stranger category offers an ethical resource, but the wrongness of murdering an unrelated civilian does not depend on the victim proving that resource to a mob. The victim has rights and dignity before anyone approves their religion. [SGGS1299]

## 3. The Real History

The November violence targeted people because they were Sikh. Courts and inquiries investigate actual acts and actual responsibility; they do not establish guilt by counting the religion of the accused's neighbours. The 2018 judgment concerns specified offences and defendants. It is an example of identifying responsibility rather than distributing it across a faith. [DELHI-2018]

## 4. The Debunk

The collective-guilt argument is morally indefensible and evidentially invalid. Explaining a crowd's stated anger does not justify its targets. The same rule applies to all communities and all assassinations. A perpetrator's religion cannot make an unrelated person a permissible substitute victim.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Condemn the assassination and the killings of Sikh civilians without making either condemnation conditional on denying the other. Name responsible actors through evidence. Refuse any account that asks an entire community to prove collective innocence. That standard would expose every population to punishment for an individual's act.

# Claim 06: Recognising Sikh suffering requires denying militant crimes

To acknowledge state abuses or the November killings, a writer must deny that militant groups killed or intimidated civilians during the Punjab conflict.

## 1. Where It Came From

An allegiance test makes victims compete. If one side committed crimes, another side's victims are treated as a distraction. The writer is pushed to select the suffering that confirms a preferred position. This produces an account in which the word civilian means only an innocent person whose death is politically convenient.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

An ethic of truthful conduct cannot permit hiding an act because the perpetrator claims to defend the community. Neither a religious label nor a resistance claim makes the deliberate killing of civilians harmless. The actual justification and the actual action must be examined separately. [SGGS62]

## 3. The Real History

The longer conflict included violence by militant organisations and abuses by state personnel. Each needs event-specific documentation. A family harmed by militants and a family harmed by unlawful state action do not cancel each other's loss. Comparing them should clarify responsibility and protection, not make recognition of one conditional on silence about the other.

## 4. The Debunk

The required-denial claim is false. One crime's existence does not disprove another crime. Nor is this a claim that every actor or institution had equal power, duties or culpability. A state has particular obligations, and that makes its actions subject to scrutiny even when a threat is real.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Allow every civilian victim to appear in the account. Identify the perpetrator and the evidence where possible. Reject the argument that a worthwhile political goal washes away a crime, and the argument that a security goal makes any means necessary. The standard must remain usable when it reaches one's own preferred side.

# Claim 07: The mass-cremation record is either invented or proof of every murder alleged

No official evidence supports unlawful cremations in Punjab; alternatively, every body mentioned in the record proves one identically established extrajudicial execution.

## 1. Where It Came From

The two claims erase different distinctions. Denial ignores a documented institutional record. The maximal claim ignores the difference between identifying a body, establishing police custody, finding a violation in its disposal and proving a particular person's criminal responsibility for the death. Those are connected questions with different evidential requirements.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

Respect for human dignity concerns the living and the dead. Religious funeral observance can explain part of the harm caused by unlawful disposal. It cannot identify a perpetrator by itself. The fact that a violation offends a religious community does not make the violation less real or make every further allegation automatically proved.

## 3. The Real History

The NHRC's 10 October 2006 account concerns cremations at three crematoria during 1984–1994. It distinguishes custody-related duty-of-care findings from violations of dignity in handling identified bodies. It also describes incomplete identification. This is an official record with a defined scope, not an exhaustive statewide register of every disappearance or death. [NHRC-CREMATIONS]

## 4. The Debunk

The invented-record claim is false. The all-bodies-one-proven-murder claim goes beyond that record. Read the categories and findings before repeating a total. A body count from a defined inquiry cannot be expanded into a statewide total or a conviction of every person accused without the additional evidence needed for those conclusions.

## 5. The Honest Picture

State what was established and what the inquiry did not decide. Keep the people whose identity remained uncertain visible without inventing their biographies. A finding about unlawful disposal is serious in its own right. It does not need to be overstated before it becomes a reason for accountability and respectful investigation.

# Claim 08: Britain directly commanded the June assault

The disclosed visit of a British military adviser in February 1984 proves that Britain planned and commanded the operation carried out in June.

## 1. Where It Came From

A documented advisory visit becomes a complete chain of operational control. The distinction between advice, contingency planning, later changes and execution is omitted. A denial can make the opposite error, declaring that no British involvement occurred because the review did not find British command of the June operation.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

The religious significance of the site makes the advisory involvement a serious historical concern. It does not answer the documentary question of who authorised what and when. The evidence required is governmental correspondence and planning records, not a scriptural passage or the emotional weight of the event.

## 3. The Real History

The UK government's 2014 review and parliamentary statement acknowledged a February advisory visit. They concluded that the advice had limited influence on the June operation, whose approach had changed. These are the UK government's account of its own records and conduct; their institutional perspective and disclosed scope should be stated. [UK-1984] [UK-STATEMENT]

## 4. The Debunk

February advice does not establish June command. The disclosed involvement should not be denied, and the government's conclusion should not be described as independent closure of every question. A stronger allegation needs the records linking it to actual planning or command. The existence of one disclosed fact does not authenticate every added caption.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Give the advisory visit its date, describe the review's conclusion and identify it as a governmental review. Explain what additional evidence would be needed to establish the stronger claim. This account can recognise why disclosure mattered without pretending the documents show more—or less—than they do.

# Claim 09: Every Sikh holds the same political position on Khalistan

All Sikhs support an independent state, or all Sikhs reject it, and the loudest rally or online poll proves the answer worldwide.

## 1. Where It Came From

A vocal group is treated as the whole population. A self-selected poll is described as representative because it produces a convenient result. Opposition is sometimes erased by calling opponents fake Sikhs; support is sometimes erased by calling every supporter an agent. Either move makes the supposed population impossible to measure honestly.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

The scripture does not give the result of a modern political opinion survey. Religious belonging, political support, peaceful advocacy and participation in violence are separate categories. An institution's statement is evidence of that institution's position; it cannot determine every Sikh's private opinion.

## 3. The Real History

Political positions vary by period, organisation and individual. To estimate prevalence, a survey must define its population, sampling method, question and fieldwork date. Diaspora participants, Punjab residents and all Sikhs worldwide are not interchangeable denominators. This chapter does not claim a global support percentage because the cited record does not establish one.

## 4. The Debunk

Both universal claims are unjustified. A rally demonstrates participation in that rally; it does not measure everyone absent. An online vote may measure respondents motivated to answer. Neither permits assigning a political belief to a stranger because they wear a turban, visit a gurdwara or live in a particular country.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Describe the actual organisation or position rather than speaking for an entire faith. Distinguish political disagreement from responsibility for a crime. The absence of a representative estimate is a reason to withhold a percentage, not permission to declare the whole community secretly unanimous.

# Claim 10: One conviction means complete justice, or delay means no findings exist

Because a prominent defendant was convicted, every family has received justice; alternatively, because justice was delayed, no serious official finding or conviction ever occurred.

## 1. Where It Came From

A headline is asked to stand for the whole aftermath. A government can point to one outcome and imply completion. A frustrated critic can describe prolonged failure as literal total absence. Both statements make it harder to see which specific cases produced findings, which remained unresolved and what survivors continued to seek.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

Truthful accounting requires acknowledging both a result and its limits. Religious remembrance is not a list of resolved cases. A person may continue to grieve and demand accountability after a conviction; another person's conviction cannot answer the murder of their relative unless that case actually covers it. [SGGS62]

## 3. The Real History

On 17 December 2018 the Delhi High Court reversed Sajjan Kumar's acquittal and convicted him in a specified case, imposing a life sentence for specified offences. That is a dated judicial finding. Nanavati's earlier report has a different status. Neither document certifies the complete present status of every case, appeal or survivor's claim. [DELHI-2018] [NANAVATI]

## 4. The Debunk

Complete justice does not follow from one conviction, and no findings ever occurred is false. A current claim about proceedings requires a current case record. This part states the historical judgment it has examined; it does not label a 2018 disposition the final 2026 status of every related proceeding.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Make a dated case-by-case ledger. Separate criminal findings, compensation, recognition, disclosure and institutional reform. They answer different needs. Survivors deserve more than a photograph of a politician announcing that the subject is closed, and they deserve more than an account that erases hard-won findings because the wider failure remains severe.

# Closing

What survives is a clear responsibility to distinguish events and evidence. November's organised targeting cannot be reduced to harmless spontaneous anger. Sikh civilians did not inherit the assassins' guilt. Official cremation findings cannot be dismissed as imaginary, but their scope and categories must remain visible. British advisory involvement was disclosed; disclosure does not establish every stronger claim of operational control.

The account must also remain open to civilians harmed by militants, to unresolved families and to correction when a newly available record changes a conclusion. There is no contradiction between recognising state duties and recognising militant crimes. The contradiction comes when a writer changes the standard according to which side the evidence may embarrass.

## Reading a record without losing the person

Begin with the event. A document headed 1984 may concern only November in Delhi, only a military operation, or a later investigation into a decade of cremations. Write down the place and period before taking a number from it. If a record covers three crematoria, do not describe its total as the total for all Punjab. If a judgment concerns five killings, do not claim it identifies every person responsible for all killings in the city.

Then identify the document's job. A witness describes what they experienced or were told. An investigating agency gathers evidence. A commission may examine patterns and recommend action. A criminal court determines charges against defendants under legal rules. A compensation decision may find a violation without convicting a named individual of murder. These differences do not arrange human suffering into levels of importance. They arrange claims about what the document establishes.

Next separate the parts of a document. A commission can reproduce an allegation before accepting, qualifying or rejecting it. A judgment can summarise counsel's argument before reaching a different conclusion. A sentence that begins the witness says should not become the court proved when it is copied into a post. Read the conclusion and the reasoning, not only the sentence that most strongly supports the result you hoped to find.

For names, preserve legal status. Alleged, found by an inquiry, charged, acquitted and convicted do different work. A survivor's account deserves careful attention without being relabelled a conviction. An acquittal does not authorise a writer to invent an alternative finding. An appeal's current status needs its current record. If the record examined is old, give its date and stop where it stops.

For totals, keep categories together. Identified bodies are not automatically identified defendants. Compensation recipients are not automatically the entire set of people who suffered. A group percentage needs its underlying list. Counting cases is not the same as counting deaths: one case can involve more than one victim, and different proceedings can concern connected facts. Dividing cases by deaths does not establish the percentage of deaths never investigated.

Finally, notice the missing voice. Institutional files preserve what an institution recorded, including its mistakes and omissions. A family may know something that never entered the first complaint. That possibility is a reason to investigate and compare evidence, not a reason to assume every institution is always false or every recollection always exact. Honest history needs both documentary discipline and room for testimony that power failed to record.

## Three documents, three different questions

Consider first the Nanavati inquiry. A reader may find testimony describing a named person's role, a discussion of the testimony and a conclusion about whether a further investigation is warranted. Those passages should not be collapsed into a single statement that a court convicted the person. The inquiry can establish a serious pattern and identify evidence requiring action while operating through a different process from a criminal trial. Read the recommendation as a recommendation and the finding as a finding. [NANAVATI]

This distinction makes the inquiry more useful, not less. It permits the reader to ask what happened after a recommendation. Was a case registered? What charges were considered? Was the evidence tested at trial? Were there appeals? Each next question requires its next record. A report's publication is a point in that chain, not proof that the chain automatically reached a completed prosecution. Equally, a missing later record does not erase the testimony and reasoning that the report actually contains.

Now consider the 17 December 2018 Delhi High Court judgment involving Sajjan Kumar. This is a concrete criminal judicial result concerning particular charges and events. It supplies a date, a court, defendants, reasoning and an order. A reader can accurately name that result without claiming that all November cases ended in the same way. The question what did this judgment decide has an answer in the judgment; the question what is every connected proceeding's current position requires a wider and more recent search. [DELHI-2018]

The time distinction matters. A writer may correctly report a 2018 conviction and then incorrectly attach a current claim about detention, appeal, sentence status or other cases that was never checked. The historical fact of the dated judgment does not automatically establish every present circumstance. In this edition, the dated finding is stated at its date. That is a deliberate boundary in the claim, not an invitation to assume a current outcome that the chapter does not document.

Finally consider the NHRC cremation proceedings. The press release cited here describes categories within a defined investigation and compensation decisions linked to particular violations. The presence of a body in an aggregate count is not by itself an adjudication of the precise cause of death. The identity, custody circumstances and treatment of remains are distinct questions. A conclusion about dignity is serious on its own terms; it should not be promoted into a different finding through an unlabelled change of category. [NHRC-CREMATIONS]

To see the arithmetic problem, imagine a record listing a hundred bodies, of which sixty are identified at one stage. It would be wrong to describe the other forty as forty named convicted murderers, forty resolved murder cases or forty proved combatants. Those are different units. It would also be wrong to say that identifying sixty establishes every circumstance in which those sixty died. This is an invented numerical example to explain categories, not a replacement figure for the actual Punjab proceedings.

The same caution applies to compensation. A family's receipt of compensation may recognise an injury or violation under a particular decision. It does not necessarily satisfy every demand for truth, prosecution, restoration or reform. A family can accept a payment and still seek further accountability. A writer should describe what the decision addressed rather than using the payment to announce that grief or the legal question is closed.

These distinctions also protect against a misleading response from an institution. An official may point to one completed procedure as though it answers every unresolved question. The reader can ask which question that procedure actually decided. A conviction, disclosure, compensation order and institutional apology can each matter while answering different things. Their existence should be acknowledged, and their limits should be acknowledged in the same account.

For families, the record is more than a statistic. A name missing from a list may represent someone whose last movements remain unknown. Accuracy therefore involves both refusing an inflated total and refusing to treat an incomplete total as evidence that nobody else suffered. The work proceeds from documented cases toward questions still requiring investigation. It does not replace either with a slogan chosen for political convenience.

This is the work behind the words accountability and remembrance. It does not promise a neat ending. It gives a reader a way to recognise an established finding, an important allegation and a question still open without confusing any of them.
