# Foreword

A community's courage does not depend on being described as invincible. Its rulers do not become just in every action because the community's scripture teaches justice. A massacre does not need a perfectly known death count before its victims deserve remembrance. These distinctions are obvious until pride or grief makes them inconvenient.

The Sikh story includes resistance, persecution, changing alliances, confederacies and a powerful kingdom. Each requires its own evidence. It is possible to recognise religious meaning in that history without using religion as an automatic explanation of every decision. Land, revenue, succession and military opportunity also mattered. A state is an institution exercising power over people, including people who do not share its ruler's faith.

This part therefore refuses two easy portraits: a spotless empire and a community reduced to violence. Neither allows us to see the people, decisions and institutions. The stronger account recognises real achievement and real limits. It also refuses inherited guilt. A seventeenth-century official, an eighteenth-century raider and a present-day neighbour are not one interchangeable person.

# Claim 01: Sikh teaching permits no use of force under any circumstances

Because Gurbani teaches compassion and shared human worth, Sikh history can contain no defensible armed resistance.

## 1. Where It Came From

Compassion is treated as a rule that no circumstance can change. Someone then concludes that the militarisation of the community must be a complete abandonment of its religious origin. The reverse error treats an armed history as proof that compassion was only a decorative phrase. Neither argument examines the conditions being claimed for force.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

The Zafarnama's familiar argument concerns force when other means have been exhausted. The condition matters as much as the permission. Kanara, ang 1299, refuses the enemy-and-stranger view of others. Read together as ethical resources, these do not make every exercise of power good; they require justification beyond hatred of a community. [SGPC-GG] [SGGS1299]

## 3. The Real History

The Gurus' period includes armed organisation and conflict, followed by the Khalsa's changing political circumstances. Early sources such as Sri Gur Sobha are evidence for that armed world, though they have their own perspective and literary purposes. A history of force requires particular events and reasons, not a claim that every event served one identical cause. [SAINAPATI]

## 4. The Debunk

The absolute prohibition is an inaccurate account of the tradition's recognised history. The opposite blank cheque for violence is equally inaccurate. Necessity, alternatives, targets and treatment of noncombatants matter. Saying that force is sometimes defensible is not saying that an offended person may decide everyone unlike them is an appropriate target.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Describe the circumstance and examine the justification. Keep religious teaching separate from a ruler's claim that the teaching authorises him. An ethical defence of force must be capable of criticising a Sikh ruler or fighter as well as an opponent. Otherwise it is simply allegiance wearing the language of principle.

# Claim 02: Banda Singh Bahadur's opponents were every Muslim

Banda's conflict with Mughal power proves that his campaign was against all Muslims as a people, and that later Muslims inherit responsibility for his death.

## 1. Where It Came From

A state conflict is compressed into two religious labels. The labels are then carried across three centuries. Political office, local alliances, class and individual action disappear. This form of storytelling supplies a permanent enemy more easily than it explains an event, which is why it remains attractive to communal propagandists.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

The scriptural resources cited in this series concern truthful conduct and shared human standing. They do not appoint a modern population as the inheritor of an old official's guilt. Neither a statement about tyranny nor an account of martyrdom licenses collective punishment of people who were not participants in the event. [SGGS62] [SGGS1299]

## 3. The Real History

Banda's movement challenged Mughal authority and local structures of power; it also exercised coercion and fought in specific places. Reports must be assessed individually, including unfavourable ones. Cunningham's nineteenth-century narrative is one historical source, not a neutral contemporary transcript of every episode. Earlier and competing accounts need comparison. [CUNNINGHAM]

## 4. The Debunk

The all-Muslims and inherited-guilt conclusions do not follow. This does not erase religious persecution or the violence of particular authorities. It asks the writer to identify them. It also refuses to hide an attack on civilians by calling its perpetrators resistance fighters. Responsibility does not become collective simply because an event was brutal.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Name the authority, the action and the evidence. Distinguish a campaign's stated goals from every act committed during it. Remember suffering without demanding hostility toward a present person because of ancestry. Historical understanding becomes weaker, not stronger, when a community's entire membership is made into one timeless accused.

# Claim 03: The Misls were one perfectly united government

The Sikh Misls always acted as a single harmonious state without rivalry, competing interests or unequal power.

## 1. Where It Came From

Collective survival is remembered, and every disagreement is removed so the achievement appears cleaner. The community's capacity to assemble becomes proof of permanent unity. Yet an assembly that sometimes reaches a common decision can coexist with competing chiefs, different territories and conflicting interests. A shared identity does not erase politics.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

Guru Panth and collective deliberation are important Sikh concepts. They are not a historical guarantee that every assembly included everyone equally or that every participant accepted every outcome. A prescription for good collective conduct must be distinguished from evidence of a particular meeting, its representation and its decisions.

## 3. The Real History

The Misls developed as political and military formations with their own leaders, followings and resources. Cooperation and rivalry both belong to the record. Later consolidation under Ranjit Singh changed the distribution of power. Calling the earlier formations a modern federation without explaining their institutions would import a constitutional model the evidence has not established. [CUNNINGHAM]

## 4. The Debunk

Perfect and permanent unity is not an accurate description. Acknowledging rivalry does not make collective resistance fictitious. It explains why consolidation occurred and what it cost. The useful account asks when interests aligned, how decisions were reached and what happened when chiefs disagreed, instead of imagining politics had disappeared inside a religious community.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Treat collective capacity as an achievement made through institutions and circumstances. Describe its limits as well. A common religious commitment can help people cooperate without making every territorial ambition identical. That distinction makes the history intelligible to a reader who has seen real organisations negotiate, disagree and sometimes act together.

# Claim 04: Ranjit Singh's kingdom was a modern liberal democracy

Because Ranjit Singh governed a religiously diverse population and is remembered for tolerance, his state had modern electoral accountability and equal political rights.

## 1. Where It Came From

An admired quality is turned into a different form of government. Tolerance becomes democracy; a respected king becomes a public servant elected by all his subjects. The flattering comparison avoids asking who appointed officials, who controlled revenue, how succession worked and whether subjects could remove the ruler.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

Sikh commitments to justice do not automatically convert a monarchy into democracy. The ruler's religious identification does not establish that the state perfectly implemented scripture. A state's structure must be described from its institutions, and its conduct assessed against the principle, rather than defining the principle as whatever the ruler happened to do.

## 3. The Real History

Ranjit Singh took Lahore in 1799 and was invested as Maharaja in 1801. His kingdom involved a court, appointed officials, an army, taxation and territorial expansion. Its administration included people from different religious backgrounds. Those features can support an account of plural government without establishing modern electoral rights. [CUNNINGHAM]

## 4. The Debunk

The democracy claim is false as a description of the institutional form. That correction does not require denying comparative tolerance or administrative achievement. A reader should be able to appreciate a ruler's strengths while recognising monarchy, hierarchy and coercion. Praise becomes less credible when it depends on giving a state institutions it did not possess.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Call it a kingdom and explain how it worked. Compare particular policies with relevant contemporaries when making a comparative claim. If the question is whether it fulfilled a present ideal of equality, state that ideal openly. Do not manufacture an election or universal citizenship to make an old state sound more admirable.

# Claim 05: A Sikh kingdom had only Sikh subjects and officials

The phrase Sikh Empire means that its population, administration and army consisted only of Sikhs, and every achievement belonged to a single religious group.

## 1. Where It Came From

The label of a ruling dynasty becomes the identity of everyone under its rule. This happens in many histories: a Muslim ruler makes every subject Muslim, or a Hindu ruler makes every institution exclusively Hindu. The label hides the work of people who do not fit the preferred story.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

The scriptural refusal to reduce human standing to a community label is relevant ethically. It does not supply a demographic table for a nineteenth-century kingdom. The population and administration must be reconstructed through the appropriate records. A religious ideal should not be used to invent either complete diversity or complete exclusivity. [SGGS1349]

## 3. The Real History

Ranjit Singh's domains included Sikh, Muslim and Hindu subjects and officials, alongside foreign military specialists. The mixture varied by territory and role. His state used Persian in administration, and its coinage invoked Sikh Gurus. A coin's inscription is evidence of political and religious presentation, not a census of its users. [CUNNINGHAM] [COIN]

## 4. The Debunk

The exclusive-population claim is wrong. This does not mean that the Sikh identity of the ruler and the state's symbols were irrelevant. It means that identity and demographic exclusivity are different statements. The people taxed, employed, enlisted or governed by a state do not disappear because the state's conventional name highlights its ruling tradition.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Credit named contributions and describe institutions accurately. Keep a political label where it is useful, then explain its limits. A more complete account can recognise Sikh sovereignty and the work of non-Sikh participants together. Neither recognition requires taking the other away.

# Claim 06: British praise of Sikh soldiers proved a biological martial race

British recruitment preferences scientifically established that Sikhs inherit a special racial capacity for courage and warfare.

## 1. Where It Came From

Imperial officials classified selected communities as martial races. Being praised by a powerful state can feel like an objective certificate of superiority, even when the certificate serves the state's recruiting needs. Later pride may preserve the flattering part while forgetting the political interests and racial assumptions of the classification.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

The religious significance of discipline, courage and service does not require a hereditary racial hierarchy. Initiation and ethical commitment are different from ancestry. A claim that military virtue is biologically restricted to a community would need independent biological evidence, not merely a religious history or an officer's opinion.

## 3. The Real History

Streets examines martial-race ideas as part of imperial culture, recruitment and masculinity. Colonial states selected recruits and then described the selected groups through categories useful to their own administration. Training, military institutions, opportunities and restrictions shaped who served. The selection policy is part of what must be explained, not a neutral experiment demonstrating innate traits. [STREETS]

## 4. The Debunk

Recruitment classification is not scientific proof of a racial essence. This correction does not diminish the courage of particular Sikh soldiers or the tradition of service. It locates those achievements in people, training, discipline and circumstances. Actual deeds do not become smaller when they stop being used to rank the biology of entire populations.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Honour service through documented actions and the conditions in which soldiers served. Ask who was recruited and why. Reject the inference that a person outside the favoured category is naturally cowardly. A community's achievements are more meaningful when they are achievements, rather than supposedly automatic outputs of ancestry.

# Claim 07: A battle's moral importance depends on an enormous exact enemy count

A heroic defence matters only if the famous ratio is literally exact, so questioning the denominator insults the defenders.

## 1. Where It Came From

Numbers make courage easy to repeat. With each retelling, a large force can become a precisely enormous force. The count then acquires the status of a sacred object: anyone requesting its source is accused of reducing the sacrifice. That protects arithmetic by placing emotion where the evidence should be.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

The ethical language of truthful living does not make exaggeration a religious duty. Narrative and martial poetry can use scale, imagery and conventional numbers to convey danger. A historian must identify whether a number is intended as a count, a rhetorical expression or a later addition, rather than assuming all numbers perform the same task. [SGGS62]

## 3. The Real History

Early accounts such as Sri Gur Sobha describe conflict but must be read as particular works with their own purposes and textual histories. Troop strength also depends on whether a figure refers to the whole campaign, a siege force or attackers present at one moment. Those are different denominators, even if every source is acting in good faith. [SAINAPATI]

## 4. The Debunk

The demand that one exact ratio be accepted is unjustified when its underlying count has not been established. This does not prove that the defenders faced an equal force. It means their danger and conduct should be described at the level the evidence supports. An unsupported precise number should not be replaced with a different invented precise number.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Use an established count where possible and a qualified description where not. Explain what the sources agree on and where they differ. The bravery of a person confronting danger survives a careful sentence about uncertain troop strength. It does not require the historian to surrender the right to check a denominator.

# Claim 08: One viral percentage measures Sikhs' entire role in Indian independence

A single unsourced percentage of executions, imprisonment or sacrifice proves the whole contribution of every community to the independence movement.

## 1. Where It Came From

A precise-looking number promises to settle a large historical comparison. Different categories are combined: people hanged in one jurisdiction, prisoners in one institution, members of one movement and everyone involved in the struggle. The smallest denominator that gives the most impressive result is then described as though it represents the whole.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

No scriptural passage supplies a twentieth-century statistical denominator. Ethical regard for sacrifice does not require turning it into a competition between births or faiths. A historical percentage must be supported by the underlying list, the classification method and the period, regardless of how inspiring the claim sounds.

## 3. The Real History

Sikh participation in movements such as Ghadar, gurdwara reform and other anticolonial activity can be studied through people and events. To make a percentage, first specify the population counted. Are names complete? Are religious identities known? Are different kinds of participation being treated as equivalent? An unanswered question here changes what the percentage can mean.

## 4. The Debunk

An unsourced universal percentage is not a reliable measure. The absence of a valid total does not erase documented contribution. Conversely, a correct percentage for one limited list does not automatically describe the entire independence movement. Reject the expansion unless the additional evidence is supplied.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Tell the specific histories and give a percentage only with its exact denominator. A reader should be able to reproduce the arithmetic. Honour sacrifice without using it to say another community contributed nothing or owes permanent political obedience. Contribution is historical work to understand, not a hereditary invoice to send to strangers.

# Claim 09: Uncertain massacre totals make the massacre uncertain

If historians differ on the number killed in a Ghallughara or another episode of mass violence, the violence itself must be fictional or exaggerated beyond recognition.

## 1. Where It Came From

Uncertainty is moved from one question to another. The count is disputed, so the event is denied. A defender may answer by insisting that one maximum figure is beyond doubt. Both reactions make a precise total carry more historical weight than it can support.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

Religious remembrance can carry the meaning of suffering and survival. It does not provide a complete administrative register of every death. A commemorative account and a historical count can be related without being identical. Taking suffering seriously requires truthful description, including the difference between an estimate and an established individual record.

## 3. The Real History

Counts of mass violence are affected by incomplete records, displaced populations, different boundaries and contemporary exaggeration or minimisation. Researchers compare sources and identify how estimates were made. An early report may establish widespread killing while leaving the exact total open. Later remembrance may preserve the event without resolving that arithmetic. [CUNNINGHAM]

## 4. The Debunk

An uncertain total does not logically imply an uncertain event. The evidence for occurrence, scale, responsibility and precise number must be assessed separately. Nor should a range be hidden when it is relevant. Honest qualification makes it harder for a denier to exploit an overstated number as a reason to reject the whole record.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Name the event, the perpetrators supported by the record and the basis for the count. Where totals differ, report that they are estimates and explain the difference. Victims do not lose their human claim to remembrance because the historian cannot produce a complete list after a destructive campaign.

# Claim 10: A ruler's religious label makes every policy religiously correct

Every policy of a Sikh ruler must be an expression of Sikh teaching, so questioning taxation, conquest or unequal treatment questions the Gurus themselves.

## 1. Where It Came From

Identification becomes endorsement. The ruler belongs to a religious community, therefore the community's highest authority supposedly approves every act. A critic can make the same error in reverse, treating a ruler's wrongdoing as proof that the scripture commanded it. Both positions remove the possibility of judging the ruler by the teaching.

## 2. What the Sikh Texts Say

Truthful conduct, sharing and refusal of hostility supply standards. They are not a list of automatic approvals for a future ruler. If a political act contradicts the standard, the contradiction needs examination. A ruler's invocation of a Guru is evidence about the ruler's presentation, not proof of the Guru's approval. [SGGS62] [SGGS1245] [SGGS1299]

## 3. The Real History

States collect revenue, organise coercion, settle disputes and pursue strategic interests. Religious language can inform those acts, justify them or conceal them. A historical inquiry examines what happened and who benefited or suffered. It must include subjects and opponents alongside the ruler's court, otherwise the archive of power becomes the whole story.

## 4. The Debunk

The automatic endorsement is invalid. It is possible to call a state Sikh in a conventional historical sense and still assess each policy critically. That is not a refusal to recognise sovereignty. It is a refusal to treat sovereignty as an exemption from the moral questions the tradition itself makes available.

## 5. The Honest Picture

Give achievement and wrongdoing their evidence. Compare particular policies rather than declaring the state either immaculate or wholly worthless. The person governed by the state matters as much as its flag. A religious community's history becomes more credible when its rulers remain accountable to the standards used to judge everyone else.

# Closing

The strongest account of Sikh political history does not need a hereditary enemy, a biological warrior caste, a fabricated percentage or a democratic constitution retrofitted to a kingdom. It needs named actors, dated institutions, careful sources and the willingness to assess one's own side.

Courage survives scrutiny. So do communal endurance and major administrative achievement. What does not survive is the claim that those achievements cancel every difficulty. A state can offer comparative religious accommodation while imposing hierarchy and taxation. A confederacy can resist persecution while experiencing rivalry. A remembered massacre can be established while its exact total remains open.
