Starter lesson reference

Country, government, Constitution.

Think of your country as a school that continues for many years. The team running the school can change. Its basic rulebook says what that team can do and what rights students have.

Country: the place and its people.

Government: the people and institutions running public affairs at a particular time.

Constitution: the basic legal framework that creates institutions, gives them powers, limits those powers, and protects rights.

The school example helps you start; it is not a complete model. A government is accountable under the Constitution, and constitutional changes require a special legal process.

Memory hook: Place and people. Running team. Basic rulebook.

Hindi bridge: Sarkar badal sakti hai, lekin nayi sarkar ko bhi Samvidhan ke niyam maanne padte hain. The government may change; the new government must still follow the Constitution.

Your turn — without looking above.After an election, a new government takes office. Does that automatically create a new country or a new Constitution? Explain why in 2–3 sentences.

Give your answer to your AI mentor. Ask it to check the reasoning, explain any mistake simply, and ask one new question. Reading this page alone is not evidence that you have learned the concept.
